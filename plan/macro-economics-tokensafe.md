## Summary: The AI Supply Network & Market Dynamics

The artificial intelligence industry functions as a highly interdependent global supply network rather than a linear supply chain. Its economic structure is currently splitting into two distinct realities: extreme monopolies at the physical layer and hyper-commoditization at the software layer.

------------------------------
## 1. The Core Stages & Dominant Players
The global AI value network is structured across five interdependent layers, each controlled by concentrated gatekeepers:

* Raw Materials & Equipment: Dominated by ASML (a virtual monopoly on EUV lithography machines required for advanced chip printing) and China (which controls upstream rare-earth mineral refining).
* Design & Fabrication: Dominated by NVIDIA (controlling up to 86% of data center AI accelerators and the proprietary CUDA ecosystem), TSMC (the absolute manufacturing bottleneck for cutting-edge chips), and SK Hynix (leading in High-Bandwidth Memory).
* Compute & Cloud Infrastructure: Dominated by hyperscalers (Microsoft Azure, AWS, Google Cloud), alongside emerging infrastructure and energy providers (Vertiv, Constellation Energy) managing the massive power and cooling grids required by data centers.
* Foundation Models: Controlled by proprietary closed-source leaders (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic) and open-weights heavyweights (Meta's Llama).
* Application & Orchestration: Dominated by software incumbents (Microsoft Copilot, Salesforce, Adobe) embedding AI into existing enterprise workflows.

------------------------------
## 2. The Structural Disruption of OpenClaw
The emergence of open-source autonomous agent frameworks like OpenClaw fundamentally disrupted this value chain by targeting vulnerabilities at the top and middle layers:

* Dismantling Software Monopolies: OpenClaw bypassed expensive corporate per-seat software licenses, allowing developers to build free, self-hosted 24/7 autonomous workflows via consumer apps (WhatsApp/Telegram).
* Upstream Resource Strain: Automated agents running infinite loops triggered a localized GPU capacity crunch and forced frontier model providers to aggressively restructure token limits and API protocols to survive continuous automated traffic.
* Security & "Clone Inflation": The decentralized community hub (ClawHub) caused an explosion of agent skills. However, giving autonomous agents execution access to files and internal systems introduced severe supply chain vulnerabilities, making them prime targets for malicious prompt-injection attacks.

------------------------------
## 3. Testing the Macroeconomic Hypothesis
Applying Michael Porter’s Diamond Model validates that the industry is heading toward a highly fragmented, cutthroat competitive environment at the application level, while power concentrates at the physical bottleneck:

* LLM Barriers to Entry (Shifting): Building a frontier model from scratch remains an elite game. However, the barrier to matching their intelligence has collapsed due to algorithmic distillation (exemplified by low-cost Chinese models like DeepSeek).
* Infrastructure Barriers to Entry (High at Scale): While small models can run locally at home, scaling enterprise-grade AI is constrained by heavy industrial factors—specifically, multi-gigawatt power grid access, hardware capital expenditure, and cooling.
* Agent Barriers to Entry (Destroyed): Open-source frameworks have made building AI agents trivial. Basic wrapper apps have lost all pricing power and are facing hyper-commoditization.

------------------------------
## 4. Future Industry Scenarios
